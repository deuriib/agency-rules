# Compensation

A commission plan is the strongest behavioural instruction a company issues, and
it is also a contract — read carefully by people who will notice the difference.

## Plans

- **Documented, signed, and versioned.** A plan that lives in a spreadsheet nobody
  signed is a dispute waiting for the first large deal.
- **No retroactive change without written approval.** Changing the plan after the
  number is known turns a contract into a negotiation.
- **The plan states what is measured, over which period, and against which
  definition.** Those definitions come from `measurement`; if they disagree, the
  rep is paid against one and reviewed against the other.
- **A plan change is announced before the period it affects**, never during it.

## SPIFFs and contests

- **Approved by finance and HR before announcement**, with the budget, the
  eligibility, and the stop date stated.
- **No off-plan payment.** A discretionary bonus handed out to fix a plan problem
  becomes the plan, and the written plan becomes theatre.
- **Every contest has a total cost ceiling and an end date.** An open-ended contest
  is an unbudgeted liability that grows with its own success.

## Clawbacks

- **Clawbacks follow the written policy and are applied consistently.** The same
  circumstance treated differently for two reps is a legal exposure, not a
  judgement call.
- **A clawback is communicated with its clause**, not asserted after the fact.
- **Commission disputes have a documented route and an owner**, so a disagreement
  never becomes an informal negotiation with whoever is closest to the number.
