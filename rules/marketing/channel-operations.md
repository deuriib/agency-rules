# Channel Operations

The channel belongs to someone else: it has its own rules, and an audience
watching how you behave on it.

## Disclosure

- **Influencer and affiliate relationships are disclosed clearly, prominently, and
  before the fold** — in the opening lines, not in a pile of hashtags at the end.
- **Disclosure follows the platform's own mechanism and the jurisdiction's law**,
  whichever is stricter.
- **A hashtag buried among thirty others is not a disclosure.** If the reader must
  expand the caption to find it, it is not prominent.
- **Use the paid-partnership label where the platform offers one.** The label is
  the disclosure.

## Accounts

- **Every brand account uses MFA** and grants the narrowest access each role needs.
- **Nobody posts as the brand from a personal account**, and nobody shares a
  login.
- **Access is revoked on departure and on role change.** A former contributor who
  can still post is an incident waiting for a bad day.
- **Who may be granted access is a deferred human decision** — see
  `detection-and-response`.

## Platform rules

- **Platform terms are respected**, including automation limits, scraping rules,
  and advertising policy.
- **No purchased followers, engagement, reviews, or comment activity.** Fake
  traction converts once and costs credibility permanently.
- **No spam, no unsolicited direct messages at scale, no engagement bait that the
  platform forbids.**
- **A platform change that breaks a rule in this set means stop posting**, not find
  a workaround.

## Publishing

- **Nothing auto-publishes unreviewed.** A scheduler is a delivery mechanism, not
  an editor.
- **Review scheduled content before it is queued, with the final asset and the
  final copy** — the queue runs at 3 a.m. with nobody watching.
- **Every scheduled post has an owner who can pull it.** A post nobody owns cannot
  be stopped.
- **Pause the queue first during a crisis**, before deciding anything else.

## Crisis

- **A protocol exists before it is needed**: who speaks, what is pre-approved, who
  escalates, and when legal joins.
- **One voice.** Two people improvising replies is how a complaint becomes a
  screenshot.
- **Never argue, never delete accurate criticism, never post while angry.** Correct
  an error quickly and plainly; a fast correction costs less than a slow defence.
- **Sensitive topics get legal review before the reply, not after.**
